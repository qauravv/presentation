### 1.4 Template Library

**Template A: Section Header**
Full-bleed palette background. Section number + title centered (H1). 1-sentence preview. Timing indicator bottom-right. Used for opening each major section.

**Template B: Content Standard**
Colored title bar (20% height). Content: 4–5 bullets MAX or 1 visual + caption. Footer: section name + slide number. ONE core idea per slide. The workhorse template (25 of 46 slides).

**Template C: Checkpoint Question**
Warm orange header bar with target icon. Question text centered, 32pt, readable from back. Format instructions at bottom (18pt muted). Processing cue in italic below. Clean, minimal — nothing competes with question.

**Template D: Checkpoint Options**
Warm orange header bar. Options as large, equal-sized cards. Identical visual weight — same dimensions, font, padding. No colors signaling right/wrong. Neutral card backgrounds. For Section 5: CRITICAL that Option B looks identical to C.

**Template E: Visual Anchor**
Large central visual dominates (60–70% of slide area). Title left-aligned (H2). Minimal text: 1 caption sentence. The visual IS the content. Used for mechanism flowchart, bacteria, eye stages, Tiktaalik, tree of life.

**Template F: Transition / Reset**
Minimal design — maximum white/cream space. Large centered text (36–48pt). No bullets, no diagrams. Carries emotional/structural weight through simplicity. Used for Lightning Reset, Consolidation Pause, Temporal Transition, Callback.

### Section 2: Darwin’s Logic — Building the Mechanism (11 slides — 13 min)
Purpose: Reconstruct Darwin’s reasoning using only ideas available in his era. Introduce the mechanism flowchart (most referenced visual). Establish the “undirected input / environmentally biased output” distinction. First checkpoint (Lamarckism vs. selection).

*   **Function:** CONCEPT
*   **Template:** B (Content)
*   **Timing:** 60 sec
*   **Compression Tag:** 🔴 CORE
*   **CONTENT:** Full text (centered, 32pt): "If traits vary, some variation is heritable, and different traits lead to different numbers of surviving offspring, then populations will change over time."
    Below (24pt): "That’s it. Now let’s unpack why that works and why it’s not as obvious as it sounds."
*   **VISUAL TREATMENT:** Template B. Navy background, cream text. Large centered text — visual emphasis on single-sentence punchline. Minimal design. No bullets, no diagrams. The words ARE the content.
*   **SPEAKER NOTES:** Give the punchline first. Students know where we’re heading, so they can organize the four pieces as you present them. Previewing the conclusion before unpacking = scaffold.

## SECTION 3: CHECKPOINT SPECIFICATIONS (DETAILED)

Design philosophy: Checkpoints are not quizzes. They are reasoning tools. The format (silent commit → small group → report range) is non-negotiable: it protects students from public self-labeling while surfacing genuine reasoning diversity.

### Checkpoint 1: Giraffe — Lamarckism vs. Selection (Section 2)
*   **Pedagogical goal:** Distinguish need-based reasoning (Lamarckism) from differential reproduction (natural selection)
*   **Format:** Silent commit (write on paper, ~30s) → groups of 3–4 (~2 min, uninterrupted sharing) → reporter shares range of reasoning
*   **Total time:** 3 minutes
*   **Misconceptions targeted:** Need-based reasoning (“organisms evolve because they need to”)
*   **Why groups of 3–4:** Richer reasoning diversity than pairs. Uninterrupted sharing prevents one person from immediately convincing group.
*   **Success indicator:** Students can articulate: Student 1 = effort causes trait; Student 2 = reproduction sorts pre-existing variation
*   **Failure protocol:** If groups struggle, spend extra 30 seconds clarifying. This is foundational.
*   **Slides:** 2.8 (Prompt) + 2.9 (Reveal)
*   **Reveal method:** Student 2: green highlight. Student 1: gray + red ✘. Key phrase: “The variation was already there. Selection sorted it.”

### Checkpoint 2: Eye — Foresight Paradox (Section 3)
*   **Pedagogical goal:** Resolve apparent contradiction between “no foresight” and “stages seem to lead toward a better eye”
*   **Format:** Silent think (30s) → neighbor discuss (90s)
*   **Total time:** 2 minutes
*   **Misconceptions targeted:** Residual teleology; progress narrative
*   **Why this format:** Simpler than Checkpoint 1 because the question is more constrained. Multiple choice gives struggling students options.
*   **Success indicator:** Students choose A and explain: each stage selected for immediate benefit, not future stages
*   **Slides:** 3.6 (Combined question + options + reveal)
*   **Reveal method:** Green border fades in around Option A. B removes selection (wrong). C smuggles teleology (wrong).

### Checkpoint 3: Convergence — Why Multiple Lines Matter (Section 4)
*   **Pedagogical goal:** Understand why convergence of independent evidence lines makes the case powerful
*   **Format:** Pairs (~90s) → square/groups of 4 (~60s) → room share (~60s)
*   **Total time:** 2.5 minutes
*   **Why open-ended:** After two multiple-choice checkpoints, open-ended tests generative reasoning, not recognition
*   **Why pair → square:** The “square” step lets pairs test reasoning against another pair. Catches errors, increases confidence for reporting.
*   **Success indicator:** Students articulate convergence = independent confirmation; one contradicting line weakens but doesn’t destroy (because others are independent)
*   **Slides:** 4.8 (Prompt) + 4.9 (Discussion Capture, optional)

### Checkpoint 4: Diagnostic — The Sophisticated Trap (Section 5)
This is the most important checkpoint in the seminar. Its purpose is not discussion but diagnosis: to surface the specific error of students who “mostly-but-not-quite understood.” Option B is deliberately designed to attract students who grasped the randomness distinction but still carry residual teleology. The visual formatting must not give it away.

*   **Pedagogical goal:** Surface the “directs toward optimal” misconception in students who mostly understood
*   **Format:** Silent individual commit (write letter on paper, 45s) → pair discuss (90s) → whole-room share (90s)
*   **Total time:** 3 minutes
*   **Why silent commit is critical:** Students must commit before social influence. If they hear others’ reasoning first, the diagnostic function is destroyed.
*   **Misconceptions targeted:** Need-based reasoning (A), residual teleology (B), progress narrative (D)
*   **Success indicator:** Students who chose B can articulate WHY it’s wrong after the reveal
*   **Slides:** 5.6 (Instructions + Options) + 5.7 (Reveal)
*   **Option B Design Rationale:** “Random mutations create variation; natural selection then directs organisms toward optimal adaptations” sounds sophisticated and uses correct vocabulary (“random mutations,” “natural selection”). The teleology is smuggled in via “directs toward optimal.” This is the exact error of partial understanding.
*   **Visual Balance Requirements (Non-Negotiable):**
    *   All 4 cards: IDENTICAL dimensions, padding, font size, border weight
    *   Neutral card backgrounds (light gray or white with subtle border)
    *   NO colors signaling right/wrong before reveal
    *   Option B and C must be visually indistinguishable in formatting
    *   If B is shorter, add line break. If C is longer, allow natural wrapping
    *   2×2 grid layout: A(top-left), B(top-right), C(bottom-left), D(bottom-right)

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Giraffe | Write + groups 3–4 | Distinguish Lamarckism vs. NS? | Foundation |
| 2 | Eye | Think + neighbor | Apply Foresight paradox? | Deepens mechanism |
| 3 | Convergence | Pairs → square → room | Evaluate Evidence quality? | Scientific reasoning |
